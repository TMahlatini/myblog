### Thinking, Fast and Slow Chapter 24: 

The engine of capitalism is sheer unbridled optimism. 

"The chances that a small business will survive for five years in the United States are about 35%. But the individuals who open such businesses do not believe that the statistics do not apply to them"

It turns out a key ingredient for a success is a touch of self-delusion; "...someone who lacks a delusional sense of significance will wilt in the face of repeated experiences of multiple small failures and rare successes..."

However, unguarded optimism has its pitfalls: it breeds overconfidence.  For entrepreneurs and businesses it can lead to competition neglect which leads to "excess entry: more competitors enter the market than the market can profitably sustain, so their average outcome is a loss."

Overconfidence can also prevent necessary self reflection: "When one has just had a door slammed in one's face by an angry homemaker, the thought 'she was an awful woman' is clearly superior to ' I am an inept salesperson'".  

My last big takeaway from this chapter was the *premortem* by Gary Klein; a partial remedy to optimism and overconfidence. It was originally developed for business teams but it works on an individual level too. Just right before committing to a big decision, business or otherwise, imagine yourself a year into the future and you have carried out your decision, and implemented everything you wanted according to your plan, but the outcome was a disaster; take some time to write a brief history of that disaster.
