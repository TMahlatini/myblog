I have been slowly going through *Thinking, Fast and Slow* by Daniel Kahneman and so far I have found it very instructive. Because my progress has been slow, I have realized that I am forgetting some of the key lessons I got in the early chapters; so, I have decided to document my progress. I will try to reflect on each chapter and capture what I found interesting. 

---
Chapter 20: 

It turns out pundits, Wall Street traders or anyone really whose profession depends on forecasting the future, overstates their skill in the matter, and undersells the sheer luck, and chance involved. All it takes is a story that is coherent and the 20/20 vision of hindsight for luck and chance to be turned into skill. 

"Confidence is a feeling, which reflects coherence of the information and the cognitive ease of processing it. It is wise to take admissions of uncertainty seriously, but declarations of high confidence mainly tell you that an individual has constructed a coherent story in his mind, not necessarily that the story is true."
