---
title: Death
published: 2026-01-04
---


I have been thinking about death a lot lately. Usually it’s sporadic, often triggered by tragic events around the world or close to me. Whenever it happened, I was careful to not let my mind wander too far because I would scare myself into hiding. Being constantly conscious of your mortality is a heavy load to carry, and now, I would like to carry it.

This past Christmas, I visited my cousins, who had just been blessed with a new baby boy. He was a couple of days old when I first held him. For the first few hours after I arrived, I just admired him from afar, I was too scared to hold him. It had been a while since I last saw a newborn baby, and his presence, forcefully, reminded me how fragile we are. It’s a miracle we make it past one. We have no control of when and how we start or end this thing called life. All you have is this moment and your awareness of it. There are no guarantees beyond that. 

When I started reflecting on this, I thought I was just afraid of the finality of death; the inescapable erasure of the totality of my existence. However, I realized there is something else at play. My fear of death is the full manifestation of my fear of failure. Failure of unrealizing my potential. The tragic thing about death is there are no redoes. Whatever experiences you have accumulated when the bell tolls, that's it, no more new convictions to remedy past failures.  

I want to prevent the scenario where I am on my deathbed, plagued with regret. My first step is redefining the popular aphorism “YOLO” by divorcing it from all the impulsivity associated with it. My chosen working definition is: You Only Live Once! Don’t let the irony distract you from the boldface. It represents more intentionality and a solemn appreciation of the fact I will only live once. 

Being able to truly appreciate the fact that tomorrow is not guaranteed, and that today might be the last day, is a big feat. I respect and envy people that have reached that summit. My wish is to reach it too, someday. I want to look at death and smile, not shrink at the thought of all my unfulfilled dreams and goals. I want to smile, an honest and sincere smile of someone who appreciated life and tried his best to never take it for granted.
