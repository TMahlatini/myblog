### Thinking, Fast and Slow: Chapt 21

Speaking on Paul Meehl's *Clinical vs Statistical Prediction* research:  "The important conclusion from this research is that an algorithm that is constructed on the back of an envelope is often good enough to compete with an optimally weighted formula, and certainly good enough to outdo expert judgement." 

Another interesting quote: "The story of a child dying because an algorithm made a mistake is more poignant than a story of the same tragedy occurring as result of human error, and the difference in emotional intensity is readily translated into moral preference"

The latter quote reminds me of the movie *"I, Robot"*. There is a scene in the movie where a robot chooses to save an adult over a child based on the probabilistic assessment which ranked the child's chances very low. The robot made a decision based on a formula that was accurate; if I remember correctly, there was probably no hope for the kid in that scene, but no matter how many times you watch it, you won't have any sympathy for the robot's decision.
